# Medical and Aesthetic Content Safety Checklist

Use this checklist for every healthcare, aesthetic, wellness, or
medical-adjacent deliverable. It supplements the workflow in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. Claim integrity

- [ ] Every clinical claim traces to a verifiable source in the evidence
      ledger.
- [ ] Claims are scoped to the population, device, protocol, and
      indication actually studied.
- [ ] Single small studies are not presented as definitive evidence.
- [ ] Manufacturer material is used only for specifications, not as sole
      proof of effectiveness.
- [ ] No implied cure, guarantee, permanence, or "risk-free" language.
- [ ] No comparative superiority claim without direct supporting
      evidence.
- [ ] Uncertainties and limitations are stated where material.

## 2. Suitability and consent

- [ ] The article explains who may benefit and who may not be suitable.
- [ ] The reader is directed to professional assessment rather than
      self-diagnosis.
- [ ] No individualized diagnosis or prescription is offered.
- [ ] Consent and privacy requirements are met for any patient material.
- [ ] Patient images, stories, or quotes have documented, specific
      consent.

## 3. Reviewer and author integrity

- [ ] The named author exists and their credentials are accurate.
- [ ] A suitably qualified clinician has reviewed medical claims.
- [ ] Reviewer notes appear only after review actually occurred.
- [ ] `NOT MEDICALLY APPROVED` is applied when review is missing.

## 4. Advertising and conversion ethics

- [ ] Before-and-after images are real, consented, and not misleading.
- [ ] Pricing, promotions, and availability are accurate.
- [ ] The CTA invites consultation without pressure or fear tactics.
- [ ] No hidden commercial relationship or undisclosed sponsorship.
- [ ] Testimonials are real, attributable, and representative.

## 5. Technical and editorial

- [ ] One H1 and a logical heading hierarchy.
- [ ] Publication and update dates are honest.
- [ ] Internal links point to real, existing pages.
- [ ] Structured data matches visible, true page content.
- [ ] Alt text describes the image without keyword stuffing.

## Adapting to another industry

To repurpose this skill outside healthcare:

1. Replace the clinical-claim rules above with the equivalent
   regulated-claim rules for the new domain (for example, financial
   advice, legal services, or supplements).
2. Keep the evidence-ledger and hard-stop structure unchanged; it is
   domain-neutral.
3. Update the reviewer requirement to the correct qualified
   professional for that domain.
4. Re-check local advertising regulations before publishing.

Never weaken the anti-fabrication rules. They are the core of the skill.
